# Learning goals

The students, David Graham and Dominic Sciarrino, need a shared vocabulary before they can do research with Dr. John Krolewski (SME) and Dr. Joaquin Carbonara (technical advisor).

The four modules are **one argument**:

> A cell’s state is an information-processing program. scRNA-seq reads a noisy projection of that program. Cancer corrupts the program and its niche. Immunity is the recognition system that can clear, ignore, or be blocked by that niche — and single-cell data is one way to look at both sides at once.

## Outcomes (students)

After the tutorial, a student should be able to:

1. Explain information flow beyond a slogan-level central dogma (transcription, regulation, why mRNA is an incomplete readout).
2. Describe a droplet scRNA-seq experiment through the count matrix, and list three failure modes (dropout, doublets, over-naming clusters).
3. Use the hallmarks of cancer as a map, and distinguish driver events from heterogeneous cell states.
4. Walk the cancer-immunity cycle, place PD-1/CTLA-4 on it, and say what “hot vs cold” TIME does and does not mean.
5. Ask the AI tutor for a mechanism, then **check** anything consequential with the SME.

## Non-goals

- Not a medical course and not clinical advice.
- Not a Scanpy/Seurat lab (that can follow; this tutorial is conceptual plus light interaction).
- Not a complete immunology textbook.
- Not Dr. Krolewski’s unpublished results.

## Estimated path

About 25–35 minutes per module, including the checkpoint quiz. The tutor is optional: quizzes grade without an OpenAI key.
